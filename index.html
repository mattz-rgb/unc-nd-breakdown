Overview

Carolina went in as a 21-point underdog, led 7-0, trailed 21-7, pulled within a point on the first play of the second half and still lost by 11. Notre Dame has now won 15 straight and 66 in a row over unranked opponents when leading at halftime.

This was a volume game rather than an efficiency game. UNC averaged 5.6 yards a play to Notre Dame's 6.4 and won third down, but it ran 51 plays to the Irish's 74 and was on the field for 37 minutes of defence. The offence did not play badly. It just rarely had the ball.

Where the game was decided

The run defence was a two-drive failure inside an otherwise ordinary day. On the 17-play drive that answered Jefferson's kick return, and the eight-play drive after it, Notre Dame ran on 20 of 25 snaps for 135 yards, 6.8 a carry. On its other 25 carries the Irish managed 106 yards, 4.2 a carry, which is almost exactly their 4.1 season average. Those two drives were the game, and between them they ate more than 13 minutes.

How the game unfolded

Carolina won the toss, chose to kick and forced a punt on Notre Dame's opening possession. The offence then went 74 yards in seven plays, with Shipp's two catches covering 57 of them, including the 44-yarder, and Easter finished it with a 6-yard touchdown. For a quarter it looked like the 21-point spread was too big.

Notre Dame answered with a seven-play, 75-yard drive capped by Faison's 28-yard touchdown, and then the game turned. On the first play of UNC's next possession Traore hit Billy as he threw, Viliamu-Asa intercepted and returned it 14 yards to the UNC 12, and Carr found Greathouse for a 12-yard touchdown on the very next snap. That was two Notre Dame touchdowns inside 20 seconds of game time. UNC's following drive ended with the Talich interception, and Notre Dame needed nine plays and 77 yards to make it 21-7 on Faison's 39-yarder. After the opening touchdown drive, Carolina's next three possessions (interception, interception, punt) produced fewer than 20 yards.

What followed was the best stretch of the day. Notre Dame drove to the UNC 1, where Jonathan Agumadu tackled Walton on second-and-goal. The defence then held on third-and-goal, and Carr's fourth-and-goal pass from the 2 fell incomplete. Carolina took the ball 97 yards in 15 plays over 7:13. Edwards was 7 for 8 for 77 yards on the drive, five of the completions went to tight ends, and Thurman's 14-yard touchdown with 21 seconds left made it 21-14 at the break.

Jefferson's 98-yard kickoff return on the first play of the second half made it 21-20, and the botched snap meant Carolina trailed by one rather than being tied. From there Notre Dame took over. It ran 17 plays and 75 yards, 13 of them runs for 73 yards, converting two fourth downs and then getting the free play after Harvey's offside on fourth-and-goal from the 3. Carr scored on a keeper and the two-point try failed (27-20). After a UNC punt, Notre Dame went 68 yards in eight plays, 62 on the ground, and James caught a 6-yard touchdown six seconds into the fourth quarter (34-20). After another punt, an 11-play, 52-yard drive ended in a 31-yard Porath field goal with 7:05 left. Carolina answered with an 11-play, 75-yard touchdown drive, Edwards to Thurman from 10 yards with 2:36 to go, but the extra point was blocked, Notre Dame recovered the onside kick and that was that.

The mistakes ledger

Add it up: two interceptions that led to 14 points, a coverage bust on the 21-7 touchdown, an offside that turned a likely field goal into a touchdown, a botched snap that cost a point and a blocked extra point that cost another. Notre Dame had no turnovers, was flagged four times for 32 yards and gave up one sack. Carolina was in this game until the middle of the third quarter, and it was the unforced errors that kept it from being closer.

Billy's job was to keep the offence steady and not turn the ball over. He did the first and not the second. He went in at 56.4% and 7.8 yards per attempt, and he was essentially that player again, but his interception rate went from 1.3% to 7.1%. The two picks cost Carolina 14 points.

They were not equal errors. The first came when Jacqawn McRoy tangled with Cristian Alvarez, stumbled, and let Boubacar Traore hit Billy as he threw. That is a protection bust and the ball ended up with Notre Dame at the UNC 12. The second was a deep shot down the left sideline that Luke Talich picked off, and that one is on the quarterback. Billy was 11 of 17 for 139 yards in the first half and 6 of 11 for 69 in the second, with no turnovers after the break.

Carr averaged 8.1 yards per attempt after coming in at 10.1, so the pass defence did take something away. He still threw four touchdowns on 29 attempts without being touched.

The half splits are worth a look. Carr was 13 of 18 for 169 yards and three touchdowns in the first half, then 8 of 11 for 65 and one in the second, when Notre Dame was winning on the ground. Billy was 11 of 17 for 139 and two touchdowns in the first half, then 6 of 11 for 69 and one in the second. Adjusting for touchdowns and interceptions, he averaged 6.4 yards per attempt to Carr's 10.8, and almost all of that gap is the two interceptions. His three rushing attempts netted 13 yards, and the one sack was the only time Notre Dame finished a rush.

I'd say the quarterback play was good enough to win a game where UNC got a normal number of snaps, and not good enough to survive two turnovers worth 14 points. He was also hit once by Traore and examined in the medical tent after that interception, and came back and played well in the second half.

Running backs

UNC snaps Benjamin Hall 23 · Jaylen McGill 16 · Demon June 12

This was the matchup I called UNC's best, and it was a non-event. Hall had 10 carries for 36 yards (3.6, long of 8), June 6 for 13 (2.2) and McGill 4 for 15 (3.8, long of 12). The backs combined for 20 carries and 64 yards. Hall came in at 6.5 a carry and the preview said four to six yards a pop would be a win; he never got there. UNC had four rushing first downs all day.

Splitting 51 snaps three ways did not help anybody find a rhythm. Hall touched the ball on 10 of his 23 snaps, and June, the goal-line back, got 6 carries on 12.

Notre Dame's running backs

Jonaz Walton had 23 carries for 120 yards (5.2), Nolan James Jr. 9 for 65 (7.2), Aneyas Williams 8 for 42 with a 24-yard long, and Carr 4 for 15 and the touchdown. Nothing was explosive. No run went longer than 24, and Notre Dame's rushing attack is not built on chunk plays. It was built on 45 carries and nobody stopping them when it mattered.

The script did not help the UNC backs. Carolina dropped back to pass on 29 of its 51 snaps (57%), about what you'd expect from a team that trailed for most of the afternoon, and the third quarter was almost a non-event on offence. That also meant the backs were asked to do very little, and what they did was not enough to change that. On the other side, the two drives I keep coming back to are the 17-play drive with 13 runs for 73 yards and the eight-play drive with 62 rushing yards. The Irish never needed a big run on either one, because the average carry was more than six yards.

Wide receivers and tight ends

UNC snaps Shipp 51 · Sadler 51 · Kekahuna 28 · Humphrey 0  |  TE Thurman 50 · Easter 15 · Sneed 9

Jordan Shipp caught 7 for 109 yards, 52% of Carolina's passing yards, and the 44-yarder was the longest play of UNC's season. The rest of the wide receiver room caught one pass. CJ Sadler, who I expected to miss the game, played every snap and finished with 1 catch for 13 yards. Trech Kekahuna played about 40% of the snaps and is not in the receiving line. Mason Humphrey did not play at all, which left a three-man rotation.

The tight ends did the scoring. Jelani Thurman had 5 for 50 and two touchdowns (14 and 10 yards), and Shamar Easter 3 for 24 and a 6-yard touchdown. Tight ends caught 8 of 17 passes and all three touchdowns. The snap counts work out to 28 snaps with three receivers and 23 with two tight ends, with no other groupings.

Notre Dame's receivers

The preview talked about Micah Gilbert, who finished with 3 catches for 34. The man who hurt UNC was Jordan Faison with 8 catches for 132 yards and two touchdowns, 109 of them in the first half. He scored from 28 and 39 yards, and the 39-yarder came on a third-and-7 where Tre Miller lost him against an eight-man pressure. That's a coverage bust, not a great throw and catch.

Faison had six catches for 109 yards by the opening minute of the second quarter and then two for 23 the rest of the way, as Notre Dame moved to the ground. Notre Dame's backs caught six passes for 40 yards between them, and Williams's three catches went for six. UNC tackled well underneath. The price was that Carr only needed to find Faison, and he found him.

The bigger issue for Carolina is what the receiving numbers say about the offence. Edwards had 26 charged targets, completed 17 (65%), and tight ends caught 47% of them. Thurman is now at 16 catches for 233 yards and three touchdowns this season. But with Sadler and Kekahuna combining for one catch on 79 snaps, if a defence takes Shipp away the offence runs out of answers quickly.

I watched the skycast, and there was almost no pressure on Carr and no penetration against the run. The official numbers back that up: 0 sacks, 0 QB hits, 2 tackles for loss for 3 yards. Xavier Lewis came in with six sacks and was the biggest individual matchup in my preview; he was on the field for 63 snaps and was a non-factor. Melkart Abou Jaoude returned from his arm injury and played 42 snaps with the same result.

It wasn't all the line's fault. Isaiah Johnson only played 25 snaps, and Belichick said afterwards that his absence hurt. Jaylen Harvey's offside on fourth-and-goal gave Carr a free play when Notre Dame had been planning to take a delay and kick a field goal, which was the worst individual play of the afternoon. On offence the line was shuffled, with Jordan Hall unable to play and Alvarez moving in, and it held up fine in protection. Edwards was sacked once and hit once, but it did not create any push.

Notre Dame's pass rush wasn't dominant either. Brewu had the one sack, Traore the hit, and Traore finished with six tackles. By the official numbers Carolina's offence faced pressure on two of 29 dropbacks (7%), and Carolina's defence on none of 29. The difference is that UNC's two came on the most important plays of the half.

The rotation was heavy. The defence used ten linemen and about 4.7 on the field per snap. Lewis, L. Jackson and Harvey played 73% to 85% of the snaps, and those three plus Abou Jaoude are the group that did not move Notre Dame's line. If there is a case that tired legs mattered, it's that a defence on the field for 74 snaps and 37 minutes had no one fresh in the second half.

Linebackers

UNC snaps McDonald 64 · Seelmann 54 · Agumadu 24 · Hickman-Collins 10

Derek McDonald led Carolina with 12 tackles, 5 of them solo, but a 12-tackle day on a defence that gave up 241 rushing yards is not the same as a good one. With a 4-2-5 base, McDonald and Peyton Seelmann were the only every-down linebackers against a team that ran 45 times on a wet field, and that is a fair question to ask. Notre Dame's backs caught six passes for 40 yards, so the underneath coverage was solid.

McDonald came in with 24 tackles through three games and now has 36. That is also the problem. A linebacker who makes 12 tackles, only five of them solo, is cleaning up after plays that have already got going. Seelmann played 54 snaps, Agumadu 24 and Timir Hickman-Collins 10.

Secondary

UNC snaps Patterson 70 · Waters 69 · G. Smith 65 · Miller 46 · Cost 45 · Dopson 23 · Bryson 13 · Butts 8

I thought the secondary did a decent job for most of the afternoon, and the numbers say mixed. Nothing went deep, with a longest completion of 39 and an 11.1 yards-per-catch average. But UNC had zero pass breakups, zero interceptions and no pressure to help, and Notre Dame scored touchdowns on four of 29 attempts. The two best throws were both uncontested.

I wasn't happy with Jaiden Patterson, particularly in the run game, where he looked like he was loafing. He played 70 of 74 snaps, the most on the defence, which makes it harder to hide. That's my read from the broadcast, and the film will say more.

Notre Dame's safeties made a difference too. Talich, who was a question mark in the preview with a wrist injury, played, picked off Billy and blocked an extra point.

Notre Dame's passing game was efficient more than explosive. The Irish got 15 of their 26 first downs through the air, on 21 completions, a 71% rate. Miller and Cost split the nickel job almost evenly (46 and 45 snaps), and Miller was the one who lost Faison. Patterson, Waters and Smith each played 65 or more snaps, and Dopson, Bryson and Butts combined for 44 between them. The depth in the defensive backfield was fine. The coverage assignments were not.

Situational football

The defence did some things right. It held Notre Dame to 4 of 11 on third down (36%), and it stopped two fourth-down attempts, including the goal-line stand before the half. It also kept the Irish from long runs and long passes, with the longest at 24 and 39. Carolina's offence converted 5 of 10 third downs (50%).

What it didn't do was win first down. Notre Dame averaged 5.4 yards a carry and had 26 first downs, 15 by pass and 11 by run, so third down rarely mattered. The Irish converted three of their five fourth-down tries. UNC's defence also forced no three-and-outs, while Notre Dame's forced two. Over the course of the game, those two numbers are what the 37:04 to 22:56 split looks like.

It was a mixed bag. Jefferson's 98-yard return was a fantastic play from a unit that Belichick said was the worst in the country last year. Outside of it, the operation was poor. Rece Verhoff's return from his eligibility wait was a bumpy one: the snap on the extra point after Jefferson's touchdown was botched and the conversion failed, and his late attempt was blocked, so he finished 2 of 3. Belichick also pointed to a punt rush and a field goal block that didn't come off. I also counted two punts where Aeron Burrell was hit, though the box score doesn't list a block.

The details: Notre Dame kicked off seven times with five touchbacks, and Walton returned the one UNC kickoff he got for 28 yards. Burrell's long was 52 and Faison returned two of his punts for 20 yards. UNC didn't put a punt inside the 20. Notre Dame's Scaife punted once, for 34. Carolina won the return-yardage battle 98 to 48, which is a reminder that Jefferson's touchdown was the whole story. Talich both intercepted a pass and blocked a kick, and Notre Dame recovered the late onside kick.

What the preview got right and wrong

  • Make Notre Dame play behind the chains: half right. UNC held the Irish to 4 of 11 on third down, but ND still had 26 first downs and went 7 of 16 across third and fourth.
  • Defensive explosiveness: right. The longest run was 24 and the longest pass 39.
  • Hall at four to six yards: wrong. He had 3.6 and a long of 8.
  • Get Shipp involved early: right. 7-109 and the 44-yarder on the opening drive.
  • Pass protection a concern: mostly wrong. One sack, and one costly bust.
  • Prediction: UNC 13, Notre Dame 34. Actual: 26-37. The margin was ten points closer than I thought, and UNC scored twice as many points as I gave them.

Final thoughts

Carolina was not blown out, and that matters. The offence averaged 5.6 yards a play, Shipp and Thurman gave it real playmakers, and Jefferson's return showed what the team can do in a single play. The goal-line stand and the 97-yard drive before the half were the best football of the afternoon.

But the game turned on things Carolina controls: an interception off a protection bust, a coverage bust on third-and-7, an offside on fourth-and-goal and a snap that went wrong. And it ended with Notre Dame running through a front that has now given up big rushing numbers two weeks in a row. The run defence is the problem I'm worried about, and it isn't a one-week fix.

If I had to name what needs to change before Pitt, it's four things. The front has to win first down so the defence isn't relying on third-down stops. The offence needs a second and third option at receiver so the defence can't just take away Shipp. The backs need to give Billy something on early downs. And the kicking operation has to clean up the snaps. None of those is a talent issue on its own, which is why the performance was frustrating rather than discouraging.

Next up is Pitt, 5-0 and atop the ACC, at noon on October 10, then Duke on October 17.

Final: UNC 26, Notre Dame 37
